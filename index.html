The daily routine issue

The real reason that shelf of supplements never gets finished

If you’ve got three or more bottles on the go and keep forgetting at least one of them, this is for you. (Hint: it isn’t the vitamins.)

5 min read · A story about the steps we barely notice
01 / The scene

It starts with good intentions. Then comes the admin.

An ordinary Tuesday. The kettle’s on, your phone is buzzing. There’s a multivitamin on the counter, a bottle that says “take with food”, and another one you meant to reorder last weekend.

One label says morning. Another has a different serving size. The one you actually need is nearly empty.

So you try to fix it. A weekly pill organiser. A reminder on your phone. A “better” bottle to replace two of the others. For a week or two, it works.

Then a busy morning comes along, one step gets skipped, then another, and the shelf quietly fills up with half-finished tubs.

You haven’t done anything wrong. You simply have more small decisions than any ordinary morning has room for.

“What if the problem isn’t the individual bottle, but the routine built around all of them?”

02 / Why the usual fixes don’t last

It isn’t the supplements. It’s all the separate steps.

A routine built one purchase at a time never comes with one set of instructions. Every new bottle adds its own timing, its own serving and its own refill date.

The usual fixes, a pill organiser, a phone reminder, one more “all-in-one” bottle, treat the symptoms. They manage the steps instead of removing them.

03 / The mechanism

The One-Step Rule: the routine you keep is the one with the fewest steps.

In a well-known study from University College London, it took around 66 days on average for a new daily action to become automatic, and anything from 18 to 254 days depending on the person.1 Every extra step in that time is another chance to skip a day. That’s why a routine with one clear action, done at the same moment each day, is so much easier to keep.
